# Conflict and Combat

Conflict occurs when there are extended scenes with apposing forces where time tracking becomes more important. Think combat in traditional TTRPGs.

## Overview

The goal of conflict is to gain three advantages.

Advantages are gained by making a skill check that must minimally beat a set scene difficulty. That skill check is then opposed by whichever forces (typically the defense) are competing with the players in that scene. If the defense fails to beat the attack, that counts as one advantage for the attackers group. Attack wins in a tie.

Until focus moves from the current player, any given skill may only be used once by each player in the scene.

All skill checks need to make narrative sense, if a player cannot think of a reasonable way to solve a problem on attack or defense that immediately counts as a failed roll for that player.

> TIP:
>
> While conflict often takes the form of a fight between two groups,
> it doesn't have to. Successes could be generated by hacking a computer, giving medical attention, presenting a good argument, driving a car, or any other action that makes sense to give the current side any advantage in this scene.

## Success and Failure

Whichever side manages to get 3 advantages first is given the narrative right to decide how they win the conflict. The
game master still has to approve of the story beat, but no additional rolls are necessary and whatever is decided
MUST be in favor of the winning side.

This could include anything from character death and imprisonment to loosing a baking contest depending on the conflict
in question.
